How the dyno works — and what it isn't

What it is

A Desktop-Dyno-style estimator, in the spirit of the one Joshua's dad used to bench-race builds on the family computer. You describe a combination of parts; it estimates crank horsepower and torque across the rev range and draws the curves.

How the math works

  1. Start from a published stock baseline — displacement, compression, factory HP and torque, redline. Each baseline cites its source in the Catalog tab.
  2. Mods multiply peak torque by documented factors: displacement (~proportional), compression (~2.8% per point), carb bore area, cam duration and lift, exhaust, heads, air cleaner. These are heuristics from published dyno lore, not physics — the exact factors are listed under each result.
  3. A torque curve is built around the estimated peak: rising from idle to the peak-torque rpm, then falling toward redline. The fall-off is calibrated per baseline so a bone-stock combo reproduces its published stock HP within ~1%.
  4. Horsepower = torque × rpm / 5252 at every point. Peak HP is read off the curve, never typed in.
  5. SAE J1349 correction converts corrected power to what the motor makes in real air (temperature and altitude). Phoenix summer heat costs real power — the dyno shows how much.

What it isn't

  • Not a dyno sheet. A real dyno measures; this estimates. Treat the numbers as "in the ballpark" — good for comparing two combos, not for bragging rights.
  • Not a tuning guide. Jetting, timing, and clearances come from the manuals, not from here.
  • Crank figures. Expect roughly 12–15% less at the rear wheel through the primary, gearbox, and belt/chain.
  • No airflow simulation — no port-flow numbers, no VE map. If a part isn't in the catalog, the dyno says so.

On specs: real numbers or nothing

This tool will never invent a torque, clearance, or spec value. Stock baselines carry their published source. Carbs and cams with unpublished flow or duration are flagged in the catalog and in the results. If you know a published number the dyno is missing, add it with its source — that's how the catalog grows.
